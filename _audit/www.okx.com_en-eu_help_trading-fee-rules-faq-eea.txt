Trading Fee Rules FAQ
Published on Sep 3, 2026 Updated on Sep 4, 2026 28 min read
This article covers everything you need to know about OKX trading fees (also referred to as transaction fees, service fees, trading commission, or trading costs), including maker and taker fee rates, spot fees, futures fees and options fees, how fees are calculated, where to find the fee schedule and the fee rate for a specific pair, how to check your current fee tier and your 30-day trading volume, what you're charged when a position is closed or liquidated, how a funding fee differs from a trading fee, and how to reduce your fees.
1. When will the maker and taker fees occur? What is the difference between maker and taker fees?
A taker order occurs when you place a trade that is immediately filled against an order already on the order book. Taker orders incur a fee based on the taker fee rate.
A maker order happens when you place an order that goes on the order book with a set price and quantity, such as a limit order that sits on the book until matched.
Maker orders are charged a fee based on the maker fee rate. Maker orders add to the order book’s liquidity, providing more depth to the market. In contrast, taker orders remove liquidity by filling orders on the order book, reducing market depth. To incentivize liquidity provision by makers, maker fees are typically lower than taker fees. For detailed rules, you can read more about our trading fee schedule .
2. Why are the fees different for market orders and limit orders?
Fees may differ because they’re based on how an order is executed , not on whether it’s a market order or a limit order. Specifically, fees depend on whether your order is executed as a taker or a maker .
Market orders usually execute immediately against existing orders in the orderbook, so they’re typically charged taker fees . In periods of low liquidity or high volatility, a market order may be partially filled, and any unfilled portion may be cancelled.
Limit orders may incur maker or taker fees , depending on execution. If a limit order matches immediately with an existing order, it’s treated as a taker order. If it’s placed on the orderbook and filled later, it’s treated as a maker order.
In short, the fee applied depends on the order’s actual execution rather than the order type.

5. How can I check the trading fees in advance?
Before you place an order, the order placement panel shows the maker and taker fee rate that currently applies to you on that trading pair, so you can estimate the cost yourself: fee = fee rate × the amount of crypto bought or sold when the order is filled.
What can't be shown in advance is the final fee amount. Your order can be filled as a maker or as a taker, it can be filled in several parts, and it can be filled at a price that differs from the price you saw when you placed it. All of this affects the fee. The exact fee charged is available as soon as the order is filled: go to Order history , select Details , and hover over Fee to see how it was calculated.
6. How are trading fees paid? Can I pay them separately?
Trading fees are taken from the trade itself. There's no separate fee balance, and there's no option to pay a fee from a different currency or with a separate payment method.The currency your fee is charged in follows the trade, not a fixed fee currency. When you buy on the order book, the fee is charged in the crypto you bought, so you receive slightly less than the quantity you ordered. When you sell, the fee is charged on the proceeds of the sale. Which currency that is depends on the direction of the trade, the product you're trading and the details of the fill, so read the fee line on the individual fill in your order history rather than assuming one fixed currency.
For spot and margin trading, the fee is: fee rate × the amount of crypto bought or sold when the order is filled. This is also why a confirmation email that shows the quantity you ordered can differ slightly from the amount that arrives in your account: the amount in your account is the amount after the fee.
7. Why is the amount I received different from what I expected? Is the spread a hidden fee?
The maker and taker fees in this article apply to order book trading on Spot, margin and Futures. If you buy or sell through the express Buy/Sell flow, or through Convert, you're quoted a single all-in price instead of a separate fee. The cost of those routes is built into the quoted price, which is why the price you're quoted to buy an asset and the price you're quoted to sell the same asset at the same moment aren't the same. That difference is the spread, not a hidden fee.
The same applies on the order book. The best available buy price and the best available sell price differ, and if your order is filled against several orders at different prices, your average fill price differs from the price at the top of the book. Where a trade also involves a currency conversion, the conversion rate applies on top.
A fee shown as 0% means no maker or taker fee is charged on that pair. It doesn't mean the amount you receive matches your estimate to the last decimal, because the fill price still varies. A change in the value of your holdings after the trade is a price movement, not a fee.
8. Does OKX charge any other trading fees?
For order book trading, the charges are the maker and taker fees described in this article. For futures, you may also pay funding fees, a forced liquidation fee charged at the taker rate of your current tier, and an expiry settlement fee of 0.01%. There's no risk fee, no markup added on top of your maker or taker rate, and no other charge applied to a fill.
We don't charge you for opening an account or for keeping an account open. Deposits, withdrawals and card transactions are priced separately from trading fees, and any fee your own bank or payment provider charges is set by them, not by us.
9. Is there a daily limit on how much I can trade?
Order book trading isn't priced or capped by a daily fee allowance. If an order of yours is limited or rejected, that comes from the order and risk rules that apply to that instrument and to your account — for example, the maximum order size for the contract, or your position and margin limits. The order panel shows the limit that applies when you place the order, and the error message tells you which one you've hit.
Limits you may have seen elsewhere aren't the same thing: Convert has its own limits, and deposits and withdrawals have their own separate limits. Neither of them applies to your order book trading.
10. How can I upgrade my VIP level and reduce trading fees?
You can upgrade your trading fee level by increasing your asset volume or trading volume.
Based on trading volume, you're divided into two levels: Regular and VIP. As a VIP user, you'll be categorized into levels based on asset volume and trading volume over the past 30 days. Your level determines the trading fees for the next trading day.
When calculating transaction fee levels, if the spot trading volume, total trading volume of perpetual and futures contracts (USDC perpetual contracts, USDC futures contracts, coin-margined perpetual contracts, and coin-margined futures contracts), options trading volume, spreads trading volume, and asset volume meet the conditions for different trading fee levels, then you will enjoy the highest trading fee discount among them.
For example, if your spot trading volume in the past 30 days is 10,000,000 USD (meeting VIP 2), your total trading volume of perpetual and futures contracts (USDC perpetual contracts, USDC futures contracts, coin-margined perpetual contracts, and coin-margined futures contracts) in the past 30 days is 200,000,000 USD (meeting VIP 3), your options trading volume in the past 30 days is 5,000,000 USD (meeting VIP 1), your spreads trading volume in the past 30 days is 150,000,000 USD (meeting VIP 2), and your asset size is 5,000,000 USD (meeting VIP 4), then you will enjoy a VIP 4 fee tier, and all tradings across all business lines are eligible for VIP 4 benefits.
For details on trading volume and asset size requirements, please refer to the Trading Fee Rules .

15. How is the trading fee calculated?
Instruments
| Calculation formulas of trading fees
|
Spot/Margin
| Trading fee of Spot/Margin = Fee rate × Amount of bought or sold crypto when order filled.
Charge rule of crypto: Fee = Fee rate × Amount of bought crypto when order was filled;
Take BTC/USDC spot as an example, assuming the current price of BTC is 20,000 USDC;
Trader A (Maker fee: 0.08%; Taker fee: 0.1%) bought 1 BTC at market price, and became a taker of this trade, so the Trading fee = 0.1% × 1 = 0.001 BTC, and A will receive 0.999 BTC after fee deducted;
Trader A sold 1 BTC at limit price, and received 20,000 USDC. Trader A became a maker of this trade, so the Trading fee = 0.08% × 20,000 = 16 USDC, and A will receive 19,984 USDC after fee deducted.
Trading fee rebate rule: Fee = Fee rate × Amount of sold crypto when order was filled;
Take BTC/USDC spot as an example, assuming the current price of BTC is 20,000 USDC;
Trader A (Maker fee: -0.002%; Taker fee: 0.025%) sold 1 BTC at limit price, and became a maker of this trade, so the Trading fee rebated to A = 0.002% × 1 = 0.00002 BTC;
Trader A bought 1 BTC with a limit order, and received 20,000 USDC. Trader A became a maker of this trade, so the Trading fee rebated to A = 0.002% × 1 × 20,000 = 0.4 USDC;
|
Futures
| Trading fee of USDC-margined perpetual and expiry futures = Fee rate × (Number of contracts × Contract Multiplier × Contract size × Fill price).
Charge rule: Trading fee of USDC-margined perpetual futures is settled in USDC and is charged when the order is filled;
Take BTCUSDC perpetual futures (Contract size is 0.01 BTC, Contract Multiplier is 1) as an example, assuming the current price of BTC is 20,000 USDC;
Trader A (Maker fee: 0.02%; Taker fee: 0.05%) bought/sold 100 contracts (1 BTC) at market price with 10x leverage, and used 2,000 USDC (0.1 BTC) as margin. Trader A became a taker of this trade, so the Trading fee = 0.05% × (100 × 1 × 0.01 × 20,000) = 10 USDC;
Trader A bought/sold 100 contracts (1 BTC) at limit price with 10x leverage, and used 2,000 USDC (0.1 BTC) as margin. Trader A became a maker of this trade, so the Trading fee = 0.02% × (100 × 1 × 0.01 × 20,000) = 4 USDC.
Trading fee of Crypto-margined perpetual and expiry futures = Fee rate × (Number of contracts × Contract Multiplier × Face value per contract / Fill price).
Charge rule: Trading fee for Crypto-margined perpetual and expiry futures is settled in the traded crypto and is charged when the order is filled;
Take BTCUSD perpetual futures (Contract size is 100 USD, Contract Multiplier is 1) as an example, assuming the market price of BTC is 20,000 USD.
Trader A (Maker fee: 0.02%; Taker fee: 0.05%) bought/sold 100 contracts (10,000 USD) at market price with 10x leverage, and uses 0.05 BTC (1,000 USD) as margin. If Trader A is the taker of this trade, the trading fee = 0.05% × (100 × 1 × 100 / 20,000) = 0.00025 BTC;
Trader A bought/sold 100 contracts (10,000 USD) at limit price with 10x leverage, and uses 0.05 BTC (1,000 USD) as a margin. If Trader A is the maker of this trade, the trading fee = 0.02% × (100 × 1 × 100 / 20,000) = 0.0001 BTC.
Forced liquidation fee: Calculated according to the taker fee of user’s current tier level.
Expiry settlement fee: 0.01% for all users regardless of tier level.
|
Options
| Trading fee of options = Min(Fee rate × Contract Multiplier × Contract size × Number of contracts, 7% × Option premium × Contract Multiplier × Contract size × Number of contracts)
Take BTCUSD options (Contract Multiplier is 0.01, Contract size is 1 BTC, Option premium is 0.05 BTC) as an example.
Trader A (Maker fee: 0.02%; Taker fee: 0.03%) bought 100 contracts of call options (notional is 1 BTC):
If trader A is the taker when order filled, so the Trading fee = Min(0.03% × 0.01 × 1 × 100, 7% × 0.05 × 0.01 × 1 × 100) = 0.0003 BTC;
If trader A is the maker when order filled, so the Trading fee = Min(0.02% × 0.01 × 1 × 100, 7% × 0.05 × 0.01 × 1 × 100) = 0.0002 BTC.
Exercise fee = Min(0.02% × Contract Multiplier × Contract size × Number of contracts, User’s taker fee rate × Contract Multiplier × Contract size × Number of contracts, 7% × Settlement value × Contract Multiplier × Contract size × Number of contracts)
Day options don’t have an exercise fee. Day options are options that don’t expire on Fridays. Only applicable to exercised options. Doesn’t apply to unexercised options.
Forced liquidation fee = Min(User’s taker fee rate × Contract Multiplier × Contract size × Number of contracts, 7% × mark price × Contract Multiplier × Contract size × Number of contracts)

Only legs that incur a trading fee are counted towards the 30-day trading volume of the corresponding instrument.

16. What’s the difference between futures opening and closing fees?
Fees are charged when an order is executed. There is no difference between the fees for opening or closing positions. Fees are collected based on the executed size of the order, corresponding to either the taker or maker fee.
17. Are fees charged for liquidation?
Forced liquidation fees are charged according to the taker fee rate of your current level.
18. Why is there a discrepancy between the profit when holding a position and after closing it?
For futures and options trading, the realized profit after closing a position is the net profit after deducting trading and funding fees. The difference between the profit when holding and after closing a position is due to these trading and funding fees.
For example, in a BTCUSDC perpetual contract, if your current position value is 20,000 USDC with a floating profit of 15 USDC, the opened trading fee is -4 USDT, the closed trading fee is -5 USDC, and the funding fee is -1 USDC. After closing the position, your realized profit will be 5 USDT (= 15 USDC – 4 USDC – 5 USDC – 1 USDC).
For margin trading, only the closed profit is recorded, without realized profit, so the profit when holding and after closing the position remains consistent.
Fees aren't the only reason your result can differ from what you expected:
Futures trading fees are calculated on the size of your position, not on the margin you put up. If you open a 20,000 USDC position at 10x leverage with 2,000 USDC of margin, your fee rate is applied to the 20,000 USDC position value, not to the 2,000 USDC of margin. The higher your leverage, the larger the amount your fee rate is applied to.
A stop-loss or take-profit order isn't on the order book until it's triggered. Once it's triggered, your order is submitted at the order price you preset when you created it, and if that's the market price it's filled at the best price then available. In a fast-moving or less liquid market, that price can be worse than your trigger price, and the difference shows up as a larger loss than the one you calculated from your trigger price.
Account-level profit figures include the unrealized profit and loss on positions that are still open, so adding up only your closed trades gives you a different number.
19. Do zero-fee pairs count towards my trading volume?
Applies to your fee tier. Zero-fee spot pairs are excluded from your 30-day spot volume, so trading them doesn't move you towards a higher VIP tier. Which pairs are excluded changes over time, so check the current rule on the trading fees page rather than working from a list you saw earlier.That rule is about your fee tier. Whether a trade counts towards a promotion or a reward is set by the terms and conditions of that specific campaign, which can define qualifying volume differently. Check the campaign's own terms rather than assuming the fee tier rule applies to it.

For futures and options trading , the realized profits in Position history include closed profits, funding fees, and trading fees, whereas the profits from Order history only include closed profits. For example, after closing the following position:
From the Order history list, the opened and closed trading fees are both -0.03 USDT, and the closed profit is +0.46 USDT.

From the Position history list, the realized profit for this position includes a closed profit of +0.46 USDT. The funding fee is 0, and the trading fee is -0.06 USDT (= Opened trading fee + Closed trading fee), so the total realized profit is +0.40 USDT (= Closed profit + Funding fee + Trading fee).

21. Why don't my trading bot's profit figures match my trade history?
A trading bot reports profit using its own accounting, while your trade history and API export record each individual fill and the realized profit or loss on it. Your trade history and API export are the records of what was actually executed on your account, and they're the data to use for your own reporting. Stopping or deleting a bot doesn't change trade history or API data that has already been recorded.
22. Where can I see my fee tier and 30-day trading volume? When do they update?
Your trading fee rate is determined by your fee tier, which is based on your 30-day trading volume and your account asset holdings.
To check your fee tier:
On the app : select the main menu > profile picture > User Center > Account Information > Trading fee tier
On the web : select Assets > My trading fees
How your tier updates:
The platform takes a daily snapshot of your trading volume and your holdings of each currency at 0:00 (UTC+8)
Fee tiers are updated daily between 4:00 AM and 6:00 AM (UTC+8)
Tiers aren't updated in real time. If your trading volume already meets the threshold, your tier is upgraded automatically once the update runs — you don't need to do anything else.
How trading volume is calculated:
Daily trading volume (USD) = quantity of the asset traded that day × that day's closing price of the asset (USDT) × that day's USDT/USD closing price
Counted: spot, margin, futures/perpetual, options, and Convert. For example, XAUT/USDT margin trading counts toward spot volume, and the USDC/USDT perpetual counts toward futures volume.
Not counted: P2P trading, and the PYUSD/USDT, USDC/USDT, RLUSD/USDT, and USDG/USDT spot pairs
The platform only provides 30-day trading volume statistics. Longer periods aren't available.
23. Can I use OKB holdings to offset trading fees?
The platform currently doesn't offer a feature that lets you offset trading fees by holding OKB. OKB is charged under the same standard fee rules as any other currency.
24. Is there a fee for adjusting leverage or adding or removing margin in isolated mode?
No. Adjusting your leverage, adding margin, or removing margin doesn't incur any fee on its own.Trading fees are only charged when an order actually fills.
25. If I just hold crypto in my account without trading, am I charged a daily fee?
No. For spot and margin trading, fees are charged only when a buy or sell order fills. Unfilled portions, cancelled orders, and simply holding assets don't incur any fee.
Note: perpetual futures positions incur a funding fee at each settlement point, but funding fees aren't trading fees.
26. Is the funding fee a trading fee?
A funding fee isn't a trading fee — they're two different charges:
Funding fees are settled between long and short traders, and the platform doesn't take any cut
When the funding rate is positive, longs pay shorts. When it's negative, shorts pay longs.
Settlement frequency varies by currency (every 8, 4, 2, or 1 hour). You can see the current rate and the countdown to the next settlement above the order book.
Funding is only settled if you hold a position at the settlement point. If you close your position before the countdown ends, you aren't part of that settlement.
Formula: funding fee = position value × funding rate
USDT-margined position value = contract face value × number of contracts × mark price
Crypto-margined position value = contract face value × number of contracts ÷ mark price
Note: if your available balance isn't enough to cover the funding fee, the system deducts it from your position margin, which pushes your liquidation price up and increases liquidation risk. We recommend keeping a sufficient available balance while you hold positions.
27. What's the "face value" in the fee formula? How does the number of contracts relate to the position size I see?
Face value is the value of the underlying asset represented by one contract. It's fixed and differs for each currency. For example, the BTC-USDT perpetual has a face value of 0.01 BTC.
Face value × number of contracts = position size. For example, if the page shows a position size of 1.0935 BTC, that corresponds to 109.35 contracts (0.01 × 109.35 = 1.0935).
To check the face value of a specific contract, select the three dots at the top-right of the trading page > Market information
So "face value × number of contracts × fill price" in the fee formula is the actual value of that fill. Fees are calculated on fill value and have no direct relationship to your leverage — Leverage only affects how large a position you can open.
28. I only placed one order — why does it show multiple fills? Was I overcharged?
You weren't overcharged. A single order may fill in parts against multiple counterparties in the market, and the order details page lists each of those fills individually.Fees are calculated separately for each fill and then totalled. The total is the same as it would be for a single fill — nothing is charged twice. No fee is charged on the unfilled portion.
29. Can trading fees that have already been charged be refunded?
Trading fees generated by filled orders aren't refundable. Fees are a cost to factor in when trading, and the platform charges them under publicly disclosed, transparent rules.
